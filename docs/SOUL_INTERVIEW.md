# Soul Interview — How to Write Your Soul Document

The Soul Document is what makes Oracle *yours*. Without it, Oracle is a capable AI assistant. With it, Oracle becomes a cognitive extension of who you are.

This document guides you through the Soul Interview — a 90-minute process to write your Soul Document from scratch.

---

## Before you start

Find 90 minutes of uninterrupted time. No meetings scheduled right after. Have something to drink.

Open `soul-document/SOUL_DOCUMENT.md.template` and answer each section as honestly as you can. The quality of Oracle's output is directly proportional to the quality of your Soul Document.

**The golden rule:** be specific. "I'm passionate about my work" is useless. "I make my best decisions after sleeping on them — never in the moment of conflict" is useful.

---

## Section 1 — Identity (15 minutes)

Start with the hardest question: **who are you, really?**

Not your job title. Not your LinkedIn headline. Who are you in the way you'd describe yourself to someone you respect, after the second glass of wine?

Questions to unlock this:
- What did you want to be when you were 12? Is there a thread connecting that to what you do now?
- What do people thank you for that you take for granted?
- What would you do if you couldn't fail — and why aren't you doing it?
- What's the thing you built that you're most proud of, and why?

For the "legacy" line: what do you want to have mattered? Not what you want to achieve — what do you want to have *mattered*?

---

## Section 2 — Decision Framework (15 minutes)

Oracle makes suggestions. You make decisions. But Oracle can suggest better if it knows how you decide.

Think of a recent decision you made well. How did you make it?
Think of a recent decision you made badly. What went wrong in the process?

Questions:
- Do you decide better alone or talking it through?
- What's the question you always forget to ask that you later wish you'd asked?
- What does "good enough to move forward" look like for you? (vs. paralysis)
- What's a decision you've been avoiding, and why?

For "what I never compromise on": these are your non-negotiables. If you're not sure what they are, think of a time someone crossed a line. What line was it?

---

## Section 3 — Communication DNA (15 minutes)

Oracle will write emails, draft messages, and produce content in your name. If it doesn't sound like you, it's useless.

Listen to yourself in different situations:
- How do you open an email to a client you like vs. one you don't?
- What's the difference between how you write to your team vs. your boss vs. a stranger?
- What phrases do you actually use? ("Let's align on...", "Quick thought:", "Honestly,")
- What phrases make you cringe when you read them? (Corporate-speak, over-formality, etc.)

For "written voice": look at 5 emails you wrote recently that you felt good about. What do they have in common?

---

## Section 4 — Cognitive Triggers (15 minutes)

This section is about energy management. Oracle can't manage your energy, but knowing your triggers helps Oracle schedule better, suggest better, and flag when you're operating outside your optimal zone.

Questions:
- What time of day do you do your best creative work?
- What type of meeting leaves you energized? What type leaves you drained?
- What's a task you keep procrastinating? Why, really?
- What does a good day feel like? What does a bad day feel like?
- What's the fastest way to get you into flow?

Be honest about what drains you. If status update meetings with no outcome drive you crazy, say so. Oracle won't schedule them.

---

## Section 5 — North Star (30 minutes)

The hardest section. Your 3-5 real goals for the year.

Not aspirations. Not "I want to grow." Specific, measurable, owned.

Process:
1. Write down everything you want to accomplish this year (don't filter, 10 minutes)
2. Circle the 5 that matter most
3. For each of those 5: what does "done" look like? What's the first concrete step?
4. Rank them: if you could only accomplish 3, which 3?

Those top 3 are your North Star. Everything else is secondary.

For each goal, Oracle needs:
- What it is (specific)
- Why it matters (motivation — what happens if you don't achieve it?)
- Where you are now (current state)
- What moves the needle (key next action Oracle can support)

---

## After the interview

Read your Soul Document out loud once. Does it sound like you? Change anything that sounds like you're performing.

Share it with someone who knows you well and ask: "Does this sound like me?" Take their feedback seriously.

Update your Soul Document every quarter. You'll be surprised how much you change in 3 months.

---

## A note on honesty

The Soul Document is private. Only Oracle reads it. You can be honest in ways you might not be in public.

The more honest you are, the more useful Oracle becomes. A Soul Document written to look good helps no one. A Soul Document written to be true helps enormously.
